The First Gathering

The Council Hall

You arrive near the edge of a gathering already in motion. No one has introduced the problem yet, but the room feels tense. Several people are staring at images of the same land — and no one agrees what changed.

Voice One The image changed overnight. That boundary was not there before.
Voice Two It could be water, shadow, damage, or movement. We should not assume.
Voice Three If we act too quickly, we may lead people the wrong way.

Ah. You made it.

Unlabeled Evidence

What do you notice?

No one tells you what the images mean. Your first task is to notice what changed before anyone explains it.

Evidence A — Earlier Image

An older view of the land before the dramatic change appeared.

Evidence B — New Image

A recent image of the same area shows a visible shift that no one has explained yet.

Evidence C — Marked Change Zone

A highlighted area shows where the land no longer matches the earlier record.

Evidence D — Conflicting Notes

Observers disagree about whether the change means danger, opportunity, damage, or movement.

The evidence is unlabeled on purpose. The Academy wants to see what you notice before anyone gives you an interpretation.
